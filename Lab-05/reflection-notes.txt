While the order's `setTimeout()` is waiting, the Node.js process is not frozen. The timer is handled by the Node.js runtime, and the JavaScript call stack remains available for other work. After the specified delay, the callback is placed in the appropriate queue and is executed when the Event Loop gets the opportunity to process it. This allows Node.js to handle other operations while waiting for the timer.

`Promise.all()` finishes faster because all the promises are started concurrently instead of waiting for one order to finish before starting the next. With three separate `await` calls, the second operation starts only after the first one completes, and the third starts after the second. With `Promise.all()`, all three operations begin at nearly the same time. Therefore, the total time is approximately equal to the longest individual operation rather than the sum of all three delays.
